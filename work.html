Summary:

Ellie Williams was never one for routine. She couldn't stay in one place for too long before restlessness began to creep under her skin, something restless and insistent urging her to leave before she could get too comfortable. Then she met a girl with whiskey-coloured eyes who smelled of coffee and caramel, and suddenly, the thought of staying didn't seem quite so unbearable.

Chapter 1

Early June
Jackson, Wyoming

Ellie Williams was beginning to think she might actually die.

She’d probably fall face-first into a pile of timber somewhere on the construction site, and someone would find her hours later when they came looking for the circular saw or something. She imagined the whole thing would be… rather embarrassing. She was only twenty-five, after all – there were workers twice her age located on the site who seemed to be handling the heat much better than she was. But then again, Ellie was never one to do well in heat.

“You’re looking a little pale there, Williams.”

Ellie lifted her head.

Owen was standing a few feet away, leaning against a stack of plasterboard. His hard hat sat crookedly on his head, and he had a half-empty bottle of water in one hand. There was a smear of dust across his cheek, and his T-shirt was dark with sweat around the collar.

He’d been working on the other side of the site for most of the day, though Ellie had noticed him wandering over a few times under the rather transparent pretence of checking on their progress. She hated when he hovered.

He took a long drink from his bottle, watching her over the rim.

“I’m fine,” Ellie said, turning back to what she had been working on.

“Didn’t say you weren’t.”

“Then why are you staring at me so hard?”

“Well, you do look a bit rough.”

She gave him a look over her shoulder. "Gee, thanks, Owen! You sure do know how to make a girl feel good about herself.”

Owen grinned.

“Seriously, though. Have you had any water?”

“Yes, Mum.”

He laughed and wandered a little closer, settling his bottle down beside her tools.

She’d been working on the same residential development since late February, and by now she knew the ins and outs of half the structures like the back of her hand. They were finishing the framing on one of the houses, while the rest of the crew was moving between the exterior walls and roof structure.

It was work she knew well enough to do without thinking too hard about it, which was fortunate, considering the state of her head.

She’d started at 7 that morning. It was now approaching 1, and the sun had settled directly above the site, turning the already unpleasant temperature into something closer to an oven ready for roasting.

Her faded grey T-shirt was sticking to her back, her hair was pulled into a particularly unsuccessful ponytail, and there was a thin layer of dust covering particularly every exposed inch of skin. Judging by Owen’s state, she couldn’t look much better.

She’d almost forgotten how hot summers in Jackson could get.

She wiped her forehead with the back of her wrist and sighed. She fucking hated Summer.

“You know, Williams…” Owen spoke up once again. “One day you’re going to have to start making friends here in Jackson. Maybe get yourself, you know… A little action on the side.”

“I have friends.”

“Oh really? Name 3 of them right now!”

Ellie paused, her eyes narrowing slightly. Owen's grin widened. He knew exactly how irritating she found this sort of thing, which was probably why he insisted on doing it every chance he got.

“Though so.”

Ellie opened her mouth to argue, then stopped. She could probably come up with a name or two if she really tried, but Owen wasn't entirely wrong. She had Abbie, sure… But that was one person.

Most of the people she really knew in Jackson anymore were the ones she worked with, and even then, she wouldn't exactly call them friends. There wasn't much point in making any, she'd told herself. She wasn't planning on staying in Jackson any longer than the job required, and getting attached to people would only make leaving more complicated.

Still, having a friendly face around wasn't such a terrible thing. Even if that friendly face happened to be Owen's, which was admittedly a fairly depressing thought. She glanced at him, then decided she might as well give him something to work with.

“Your grandma, your mum and… Oh, your sister!”

“What?”

“My friends!”

“Fuck you,” he said, laughing.

“Oh, your sister and I are great friends…” she continued, drawing out every vowel with an exaggerated wink.

Owen snorted. “Yeah? Since when?”

“Last night, actually. Lovely girl. We had a really nice time.”

“Shut up.”

“No, seriously. She was saying some very nice things about me.” Ellie paused, then put on a breathy, over-the-top voice. “Oh, yeah, yeah, keep going. God, yes!”

“You’re so fucking weird, Williams!” He threw a rag at her, which she narrowly dodged, laughing.

“You should consider coming with me and the rest at the Blue Stool on Friday night.”

“I’ll think about it…”

Owen gave her a smile that seemed far too genuine to belong to him and left.

After a little while, Ellie reached for her water bottle. She unscrewed the cap; it was empty. She turned it upside down, and two pathetic drops landed in her palm.

“Fantastic.”

Across the street, a small cafe sat between a florist and a narrow little bookshop. It had been there since before, although she’d never paid it much attention. She’d noticed the large front windows and the colourful sign above the entrance. It had a little outdoor seating area with pink and white flowers that always seemed to have someone occupying it.

Mostly. She’d noticed the smell.

Coffee…

Every. Single. Day.

The place practically exhaled it. Even from across the street, Ellie could occasionally catch the bitter scent drifting out whenever someone opened the food. It was enough to put her off her lunch.

She hated coffee.

The smell alone was enough to put her off, and the taste was somehow even worse. She couldn't understand the obsession with it. The entire culture surrounding coffee seemed like some sort of fucked-up cult, with people happily spending half their pay cheque on what essentially tasted like burnt dirt. It was ridiculous. Almost as bad as matcha.

She'd tried it before, naturally. A handful of times throughout her life, mostly because people insisted she hadn't found the right one yet.

Nope. She didn’t like coffee.

Unfortunately, the cafe also happened to be the closest place to buy a cold drink.

Ellie looked at the empty bottle in her hand again, then at the cafe.

“Fuck it.”

The little bell above the cafe door announced her arrival, and she shivered as if she were entering a haunted house. Besides the horrible coffee smell, the second thing Ellie noticed was the temperature. Air conditioning… Beautiful, glorious air conditioning. For a moment she wondered if she could get over the smell and stay there just a little longer.

She looked around the shop. Espresso machines hissed behind the counter, while a tall man with a rather impressive moustace was steaming milk. There were a handful of customers scattered throughout the booths and tables, some working on laptops and others chatting quietly over drinks.

She made her way towards the counter.

"Hi! I'll be with you in just a second."

The voice came from somewhere behind the espresso machine.

Ellie glanced towards it, trying to get a better look at whoever had spoken. There was a brief clatter of ceramic, followed by the hiss of the steam wand. A moment later, someone appeared from behind the machine, setting a small glass on the counter.

It was a girl.

She was busy finishing a drink, her attention fixed on the glass as she carefully poured something into it. Her dark hair was pulled loosely away from her face, although a few strands had escaped and were falling around her cheeks. She wore a faded green apron over a white T-shirt, and a delicate gold necklace rested against her collarbone.

There was something particularly irritating about how pretty she was. Not that Ellie had any intention of commenting on it, of course.

She looked away.

"Sorry about that." The girl was looking at her now.

Up close, her eyes were even more distracting. They were a warm shade of brown, with little flecks of gold that caught the afternoon light whenever she moved. Ellie thought they were the sort of eyes someone might write a particularly embarrassing love song about.

Nope, not&#x20;&#x6C;ooking.

"Hi," the girl said, smiling.

"Hey."

A pause.

"Can I get you anything?"

"Water."

“One water,” The girl nodded, her fingers already moving towards the register. “What else?”

“Uh, just water.”

The girl blinked.

“Just water?”

“Yeah, just water.”

The girl's eyes flickered towards the menu, then back to her.

“Okay. Still or sparkling?”

“Whatever’s cold.”

"Right, very exciting. I'll see what I can do."

Ellie frowned slightly.

"What?"

"Nothing."

The girl turned towards the refrigerator beneath the counter, reaching for a bottle. Ellie took the opportunity to look around the café again, mostly because maintaining eye contact had suddenly become unexpectedly difficult.

"Here you go."

Ellie turned back as the girl placed a bottle of water on the counter. Condensation had already begun collecting along its sides.

"That'll be a dollar."

Ellie reached into her back pocket for her wallet, pulling out a note and sliding it across the counter.

"You're working across the street, aren't you?"

Ellie looked up.

"Sorry?"

The girl gestured towards the large front windows, through which the construction site was visible on the other side of the road.

"The construction site. I've seen you coming and going."

"Oh, yeah."

"You're one of the builders?"

"Contractor."

"Right… Contractor,” the girl said, chewing her lip. "What's the difference?"

Ellie shrugged, handing her the note. "It's not particularly interesting, trust me."

"Well, now I'm curious."

"Probably shouldn't be."

That earned her a small chuckle. The girl handed her the change, and Ellie tucked her wallet back into her pocket.

“Thanks for the water.”

“No problem, stay safe out there!”

Ellie gave her a small nod and picked up the bottle. She was already turning towards the door when the girl spoke again.

“Wait!”

Ellie looked over her shoulder. The girl was watching her, one hand resting against the counter.

“Would you like to try an iced coffee?”

Ellie stared at her. "What?"

"An iced coffee." Dina gestured towards the machine behind her. "It's boiling outside. Thought you might appreciate something a little more refreshing."

Ellie lifted the bottle in her hand. "I have water."

Dina pressed her lips together, looking down at the bottle before returning her attention to Ellie. There was a small crease between her eyebrows, as though she were trying to work out how someone could dare walk into a coffee shop on a day like this and leave with nothing but a bottle of water.

“Yeah, but that’s not really a fun drink, is it?”

"I guess… But it's been doing a pretty good job so far."

"Come on. Just say yes. I promise it's good; I’ve been practising for weeks."

"Well, I appreciate the thought."

"But?"

Ellie gave her a faint smile. "But I don't drink coffee."

The girl's eyebrows rose.

"At all?"

"No."

The girl threw one arm across her forehead and leaned backwards, putting on such a dramatic display of disappointment that Ellie couldn't help watching.

"God, that wounds me. I think I love coffee almost as much as I love my cat."

Ellie tilted her head.

"That's a lot of love to have for something that tastes like it was collected from a muddy puddle."

Dina's arm dropped. “Excuse me? Well, that’s just untrue. That's a horrible thing to say about coffee.”

“Is it? Does it matter?”

"Yes, actually. Very much. Because I love coffee!”

Ellie grinned. “I don’t think that argument is going to stand very well at a court of law there, darling.”

The girl laughed – it was a nice laugh. A really, really&#x20;&#x6E;ice laugh. She thought she could listen to it for hours. It was a little louder than she expected, but it was warm and genuine. The corners of her eyes crinkled, and for a second she looked so genuinely pleased with herself that Ellie found she was smiling too.

She hadn't really meant to.

"You can't hate coffee," Dina insisted, recovering herself.

“Why not?”

"Well, for starters, you haven't tried mine."

"I don't think that's going to convince me."

The girl leaned forward slightly, resting her elbows on the counter. She seemed to be studying Ellie now, her expression a little more curious than it had been a moment ago.

“What’s your name, anyway?”

“Ellie.”

"Ellie," Dina repeated, as though trying it out. She smiled. "I'm Dina."

She reached across the counter, offering her hand. Ellie glanced at it before taking it. It was a slightly awkward angle, with the counter between them, but she leaned forward enough to meet her halfway.

"Nice to meet you."

"Likewise."

Dina's hand was warm against hers. The handshake lasted barely a second, though Ellie was suddenly far too conscious of the contact. She pulled away and reached for her water bottle, twisting the cap between her fingers.

"Anyway," she said, clearing her throat. "Thanks for the water."

"Of course."

Ellie shifted the bottle into her other hand. She really ought to leave. She had been away from the site long enough already, and Owen would inevitably have something to say if he noticed she'd disappeared.

Dina, meanwhile, seemed in no particular hurry to let the conversation end.

"Offer still stands, by the way."

"The coffee?"

"Mm-hmm. Next time you're around, I'll make you one on the house."

"You're very confident I'll change my mind."

“Absolutely.”

“I think you just want to keep me around…” Ellie said, jokingly.

“Would that be such a bad thing?”

Ellie gave her a doubtful look, which only seemed to amuse Dina further.

"Besides," Dina added, "I can't have you walking around with such a terrible opinion of coffee. It's bad for my business."

Ellie held her gaze a moment longer. "Not a chance."

“We’ll see.”

The little bell above the entrance chimed as she stepped outside. The heat hit her almost immediately, wrapping itself around her as she pushed the door shut behind her. She stood there for a moment, the cool condensation from the water bottle gathering against her palm, before taking a long drink.

Across the street, everything was exactly as she'd left it. Except, apparently, she'd managed to spend fifteen minutes talking to a girl who seemed to make it her mission to change her mind about coffee.

Dina…

Ellie smiled at the thought of her name, and she shook her head.

She was an idiot.

An absolute idiot.

And, rather inconveniently, she was already thinking about going back.
